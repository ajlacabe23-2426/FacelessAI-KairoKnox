# A Kairo Knox research session (Episodes 1–3)

The creator enjoys doing the research personally. This playbook preserves that part of the project. Assistants can organize questions and notes; they should not claim the creator has read a paper or approved a finding.

## Session agenda (30–45 minutes)
1. **Question (5 min):** Write one claim that would make an interesting episode. Try writing a disconfirming question too.
2. **Original evidence (10–15 min):** Open the paper, research report, or original incident disclosure. Separate event date, publication date, update date, tested model and real-world setting.
3. **Methods and limits (10 min):** What was measured or observed? How were examples sampled? Is the outcome produced in a lab, a benchmark, or in public usage? What cannot be inferred?
4. **Cross-check (5–10 min):** Find a different credible account or related paper. Look specifically for limitations, mitigation, and contradictory findings.
5. **Make the call (5 min):** Candidate topic / research further / ready to write / hold. Editorial decisions belong to the creator.

## Evidence grades
- **Observed:** directly documented experiment or incident; match exactly what the original report states.
- **Measured:** quantitative finding, tied to the tested data and model versions.
- **Reported:** a provider's account of its own observations; attribute it and consider its perspective.
- **Interpretation:** researchers' explanation; not interchangeable with what was directly observed.
- **Editorial hypothesis:** our question or inference; must sound like a question, not an established fact.

## Commit rules
Research notes are public until the repository is made private. Store only public-source summaries and bibliographic links. Do not check in copyrighted full texts, unpublished assets, confidential credentials, private analytics or raw video masters.

For each episode, make the script and claim ledger traceable. Do not mark `approved`, `produced` or `published` merely because CI passes. A passing automated check confirms structure, not truth.
