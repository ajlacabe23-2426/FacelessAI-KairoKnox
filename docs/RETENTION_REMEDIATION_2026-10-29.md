Audit date: 2026-10-29 | Proposed editorial controls; NOT a claim that videos were edited, re-rendered, or published.

## Editorial diagnosis
**Primary observed issue:** weak average watch time / low normalized retention. **Secondary issue:** existing Threads audience is highly responsive to entertainment and informal discussion, much less to video announcements. Candidate causes to test: delayed payoff, abstract framing, insufficient visually demonstrated actions, heavy narrator exposition, and generic high-level questions. Do not assert these as proven causes before a clip-by-clip review.

## Revised audience promise
Kairo Knox is the masked analyst who reveals **what an invisible technology does, why it affects you, and what to look for next**.
Lead with a human-scale problem: money, work, privacy, games, marketing, consumer products, software failure, or surprising robotic behavior. Back up extraordinary stories with evidence. The mask supports story recognition but must never delay the viewer's understanding of the subject.

## Required short-form story arc, target 22–36 seconds initially (test length against results)
0.0–1.5 s — **Proof-first / consequence-first cold open:** show striking original/cleared visual plus a truthful 6–10 word line about a concrete consequence. No standalone logo sequence or 'Welcome back' intro. Introduce character naturally.
1.5–5 s — **The why-watch promise:** give the viewer one answer, useful experiment, or surprising reveal to stay for.
5–14 s — **Visible cause/evidence:** original footage, licensed clip with specific rights, genuinely demonstrable UI or a plainly labeled illustration; don't make vague AI motion the whole scene.
14–25 s — **Reveal + concrete takeaway:** what happened; evidence vs simulation; one actionable understanding, limitation or diagnostic.
25–34 s — **One answerable, non-generic prompt:** 'Which permission would you revoke first?' rather than 'What do you think of AI?' Optional short loop-friendly ending; do not mislead.
Run 12–20-second cuts for a concept with one genuinely fast reveal; longer only when a demonstration truly requires it. These are **pilot design targets, not established algorithm thresholds**.

## Visual, audio, and scripting QA gates
- The topic is recognizable without captions within 2 seconds via subject/object + main verbal line. Never use several seconds of futuristic establishing shots before the payoff.
- Each shot must add new information, show a changed state, or deliberately build suspense. Compare 1.5–3-second shot lengths as an **editing experiment**, not a mandatory pace; no strobing or aggressive flash effects.
- A visually meaningful primary artifact is present: diagram, robot task, visible button/action, input/output, first-party product demo, before/after, or clearly labeled scenario illustration.
- At most one new key claim per beat. Remove generalized lines such as 'AI is changing everything,' 'the future is here,' and repetitive 'but what if?' openers.
- Sound design: clean intelligible narration; voice variation in emphasis; music never competes with the voice. Use only properly licensed/original audio. Avoid clickbait alarms and excessive jump-scare effects.
- Large, high-contrast captions; don't cover the focal demonstration with text. 9:16, 1080×1920 as appropriate, phone-safe layout. Title card must have a recognizable topic, not solely a series number.
- Preserve Kairo's established mask, design and voice profile. Avoid turning him into a generic commentator or overproduced avatar dance. An occasional dry, accurate joke is okay when relevant.
- Clearly identify simulated or recreated demonstrations on-screen; distinguish original footage, illustration, reported research, and live verification. Link primary sources in description where suitable.
- Originality and rights review: no assumed permission to clip from games, commercials, entertainment or creator videos. Commentary and attribution do not automatically clear rights.
- User must see and approve finished playable MP4 and ZIP before public posting. Never schedule/publish based on this document alone.

## Four editable content lanes, keeping existing cadence
1. **Beneath the System / Core:** a single strange, verified behavior, conflict or consequential AI/cyber/robotics story. Evidence-first and consequence-first.
2. **Micro Learning:** actual bounded task with setup → visible action → observable result → 'what it doesn't prove'; maintain **one Micro Learning for every two Core Episodes**.
3. **Signal Cuts:** a new human-world story in business, ads, recommendation systems, games, entertainment and financial literacy linked to **one real AI/algorithm mechanism**; use original visuals/cleared rights. Begin with no more than one pilot weekly.
4. **Threads Debate Drops:** separate text-first audience conversations about entertainment, games, business, design, AI and tradeoffs. Do not auto-crosspost every Kairo video.

## Ready-to-produce pilot A: recommendation-system Signal Cut (original explanatory simulation)
**Working title:** 'Your Feed Isn't Reading Your Mind. It's Reacting to You.'
**Audience:** ordinary social-media users; no technical prerequisite.
**Visual premise:** an original mock content feed with three fictitious interests (games, food, gadgets) and a transparent demonstration of simple ranking scores. Mark **ILLUSTRATIVE MODEL — NOT A LIVE PLATFORM ALGORITHM** during the mechanism example. This shows an idea, not any company's proprietary system.

| Time | Picture | Kairo narration / on-screen claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 s | Mock feed changes after a pause; visually clear before/after | 'Your feed changed after one tiny decision.' |
| 2–6 s | Rewind to three post options and a pause indicator | 'It doesn't need to read your mind. It observes signals.' |
| 6–13 s | Original illustrative scores update when viewer watches one sample longer | 'A recommendation system can use behavior to decide what to rank next.' |
| 13–21 s | Side-by-side: two alternative feeds from different sample interactions | 'That's useful when it discovers what you want—and limiting if it keeps showing the same kind of thing.' |
| 21–29 s | On-screen 'INTEREST ≠ IDENTITY' note; show reset/variety control as illustrative | 'One pause isn't a personality test. These systems can be wrong.' |
| 29–33 s | Mask overlays split-screen feeds | 'Would you rather control your feed—or let it learn from you?' |

**Fact check:** confirm general claims in reliable documentation/research before narration; do not imply a specific platform has this exact ranking formula. No real brand UI, logos or copyrighted clips required.
**Status:** outline only. No MP4, ZIP, credits spent, scheduling or publication.

## Ready-to-propose pilot B: consequence-first robotics Core
**Hook:** 'The surprising part wasn't that AI moved a robot. It's which permission let it act.'
Make the first frame a rights-cleared real experiment clip or an explicit original schematic if footage clearance is unavailable. Verify source and precise experimental boundary. Deliver a diagram showing **proposal → policy check → human approval → permitted action**. Do not misrepresent experimental evidence as a factory incident. End 'Which step should never be skipped?' Avoid repeating episode 011's story unless there is substantively new evidence, process or visual teaching value.

## Ready-to-propose pilot C: Micro Learning with visible verification
**Hook:** 'This app looks fine—until you switch accounts.' Show two *fictional* sessions and an offline server-side authorization check: denied vs allowed, with no real-user/private data. End 'A hidden button isn't an access-control test.' Ensure this materially extends the prior Micro Learning 005 (e.g., refresh + expired session) rather than duplicating it. Label simulation. Build safe viewer learning, not exploitation walkthroughs.

## Test plan: six releases, not a claim of confirmed improvement
- Baseline cohort: the 15 Instagram Reel records above. Compare posts only at similar ages (e.g., 48h and seven days) and within format/length as practical. Keep topics and cadence recorded.
- Test 1 (two new clips): stronger cold open and visible result; keep length near previous median if known; report watch seconds and percent viewed. Pre-register hypothesis: median retention rises versus comparable baseline, while meaningful interactions do not fall.
- Test 2 (next two): compare proof-first vs consequence-first opening; change only the opening presentation, not entirely unrelated topic and length. If posting both variants, space and distinguish content rather than spamming duplicates.
- Test 3 (next two): revise question / usefulness with saves, shares and substantive replies as secondary outcomes. Record what was said before publication.
- Per post: platform, video ID, approved final file, topic, length, series, opener style, first demonstration timestamp, post date, views, reach, 3-second hold / 'stayed to watch' where available, avg watch seconds, avg % viewed/retention, completion where available, likes, meaningful replies, shares, saves, follower change if attributable, sources, edit changes.
- Review after 24h, 48h, 7d. A directional success signal is **improved median retention, then improved shares/saves or substantive discussion** across comparable posts. No guarantee of virality; do not declare success from one outlier.
- If avg watch remains under ~5 s or normalized retention remains low despite stronger cold opens, inspect source media and native retention graph to determine exact drop-off, and consider a sharper 12–20-second experiment with a single visual payoff. This is a diagnostic trigger rather than a platform benchmark.
- **Metricool coverage blocker:** check whether TikTok data and the intended Kairo YouTube channel actually connect to the correct brand; obtain native YouTube Shorts 'chose to view' and retention if possible. Missing analytics remain N/A, not zero.
- **Personal Threads policy:** keep videos rare, conversations original and relevant; watch replies by entertainment/topic bucket. Don't misread the personal audience's tastes as a universal verdict on the Kairo project.

## Distinctive Kairo retention device to test
Make each episode a **'System Reveal'**: first show an outcome, then reveal the mechanism behind it, and close with one practical question. Add a subtle recurring visual glyph/tiny 'System Reveal' title *after* the hook, never as a full-screen slow intro. Keep it optional until the first six-post pilot is reviewed.
