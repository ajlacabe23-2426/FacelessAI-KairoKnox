# Kairo Knox — Creator-Informed Production Standard (Pilot)
Date: 2026-10-29 | Status: proposed editorial standard on review branch; **no videos modified, scheduled or published**.

## Evidence categories
- **Official platform guidance (ads):** TikTok's [Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/creative-codes) recommends hook → body → close, a strong immediate value proposition, dynamic visuals and sound. These guidelines were designed for advertisements and do not establish guaranteed organic-video performance.
- **Official creator analytics:** [YouTube's content performance guide](https://support.google.com/youtubecreatorstudio/answer/12220281) distinguishes engaged views, 'stayed to watch,' average view duration and average percentage viewed. Its Aug 24, 2026 view-count update means raw views alone do not indicate engagement; compare metrics within the same era and platform.
- **Creator-reported practices / observations, not their private retention results:** [Fireship](https://fireship.dev/about) describes fast, approachable developer explanations and levity. [Cleo Abram](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-creator/2026/04/22/creator-qa-cleo-abram/) emphasizes choosing future-relevant stories and making them understandable to a broad public. [Veritasium / Derek Muller](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-youtube-star-derek-muller-of-veritasium-is-challenging-scientific/) prompts viewers to confront a misconception and experience a revelation. [Mark Rober](https://design.mit.edu/news/a-q-and-a-with-engineer-educator-and-youtuber-mark-rober) seeks broadly accessible, punchy engineering stories.
- **Current Kairo evidence:** connected Metricool records summarized in RETENTION_REMEDIATION_2026-10-29.md. We do **not** know other creators' actual retention curves. No causal growth guarantee.

## New audience promise
**Kairo Knox uncovers what a system is doing, why it matters in everyday life, and how you can recognize or test it.** The mask represents the analyst. The content must be understandable without knowing his backstory.

## Storytelling recipe: SYSTEM REVEAL
1. **0–2 s: visible outcome + specific statement.** Start on a robot action, input/output, surprising original illustration, personal consequence or an authentic before/after. No channel logo, generic sci-fi establishing shot or episode number as the opening. The first sentence should be concrete and truthful.
2. **2–6 s: tension or prediction.** What normal assumption might be wrong? What would the viewer choose? Give a clear reason to stay for a *specific* reveal.
3. **6–18 s: demonstrate the mechanism.** At least one meaningful change on screen, grounded evidence, or an original diagram. Label illustrative simulations. Make edits serve clarity; test pace rather than imposing a rigid cut interval.
4. **18–27 s: resolution and one memorable lesson.** Deliver the promised answer and, if useful, one short dry joke directly related to the concept. No forced joke every video.
5. **27–35 s: targeted audience choice.** Ask one answerable question with genuine alternatives, or end with an optional concrete viewer experiment. Don't manufacture outrage, exaggerate evidence or use empty 'thoughts?' prompts.
6. Some topics should be 12–20 s; longer demonstrations may need more time. **Length is an experiment, not an algorithm trick**. Every second should have a purpose.

## Borrow a method, not an identity
- **Fireship method:** plain English + specific technical insight + precise, sometimes humorous shorthand. **Kairo translation:** a clever, accurate analogy; avoid rushed jargon overload.
- **Cleo Abram method:** broad human stakes + visible explanation + clear editorial mission. **Kairo translation:** show what changes for a person (privacy, gaming, job, business, money, or creativity) before unpacking the mechanism.
- **Veritasium method:** misconception → viewer predicts → test/visual evidence → reveal. **Kairo translation:** use safe offline demos or well-sourced research examples and invite a prediction before revealing results.
- **Mark Rober method:** experiment/game with a visible goal and satisfying outcome. **Kairo translation:** clear rules, original footage or prototype, progressive stakes, observable result.
- **Do not clone** creator voices, scripts, distinctive visuals, intro sequences, or copyrighted footage.

## 'Human appeal' without changing the avatar
- Write for an actual viewer: 'Your shopping feed...', 'Your favorite game...', 'Your business's customer inbox...', rather than abstract 'AI is transforming society.'
- Allow Kairo to have grounded opinions and learn in public: 'Here's what surprised me,' 'I expected X; the test showed Y.' Use only when genuine.
- Humor examples are hypothetical one-liners, not guaranteed copy: 'A permission prompt is not a force field'; 'The algorithm is guessing, not reading your diary.' Keep accuracy, empathy and topic relevance.
- Don't require the creator to show their face; don't add random attractive presenters merely as an attention trick. Keep recognizable Kairo identity.
- Avoid broad claims such as 'this AI secretly controls your life' without sources. Avoid faked social proof or manufactured urgency.

## Concrete before → after script examples
**Abstract, low-stakes:** 'AI agents are becoming more autonomous. Should we trust them?'
**Specific, teachable:** 'This fictional AI assistant can answer a question. But should it also get permission to delete a file? Watch what changes when we put one approval step in the way.' [Show a **labeled offline** allowed/blocked simulation.] 'Autonomy is not the same as authorization. Where would you require approval?'

**Generic:** 'Algorithms are everywhere, changing what we see online.'
**Specific, relatable:** 'You stop on one clip. What might a recommendation system learn from that pause? Let's test a simplified example.' [Show **original illustrative model**, not a claim about a particular proprietary feed.] 'One action is a signal, not your entire personality. Should apps explain the signals they use?'

**Concept-first lesson:** 'Server-side authorization prevents cross-account data access.'
**Prediction-led:** 'Two fictional users. One saved invoice. If I switch accounts, should the other invoice still load?' [Show safe, isolated test and result.] 'Hiding a button is not the same as enforcing ownership on the server.'

## Required topic-pitch and script review rubric (0–2 points each; 14 possible)
- **Hook / immediate clarity:** 0 vague; 1 understandable; 2 consequence shown by second two.
- **Viewer relevance:** 0 purely abstract; 1 niche relevance; 2 everyday human stakes.
- **Payoff:** 0 never pays off; 1 partial; 2 demonstrable answer / reveal.
- **Teachability:** 0 no learning; 1 concept; 2 applicable insight or safe demonstration.
- **Personality / emotional range:** 0 mechanical; 1 natural delivery; 2 precise surprise, empathy or relevant humor without distraction.
- **Visual credibility:** 0 decoration or misleading visual; 1 useful illustration; 2 original/cleared demo tightly tied to narration and properly labeled.
- **Platform/measurement readiness:** 0 copied everywhere; 1 tailored; 2 unique titles/captions, safe areas, explicit test hypothesis and metrics plan.

**Hard stops independent of score:** fabricated proof; unsupported claims; unlicensed material; unsafe demonstrations; private/confidential details; missing final user approval. **Suggested 11/14 script quality threshold** before spending credits, but do not suppress promising unconventional ideas automatically; human review can override with documented reason.

## Preproduction: two-openings exercise
For each pilot, write:
- Hook A: *Consequence-first*. A specific, plainly understood result appears before explanation.
- Hook B: *Prediction-first*. Ask audience to predict a result that will genuinely be revealed.
Select the stronger hook on script review; if testing two versions after approval, compare the same topic and comparable length, not unrelated posts at unrelated ages.

## Final QC before user review
- Read first line aloud without images: instantly understandable?
- Watch muted at 2 seconds: subject and consequence visible?
- At 10 seconds: has anything **actually happened on screen**, rather than Kairo's avatar talking over decorative motion?
- At the end: is the original promise fulfilled?
- Does one short phrase clearly express what the viewer learned?
- Are satire, reenactments, source footage and illustrative model labels unambiguous?
- Is this a distinctive Kairo video, not a generic AI news slideshow?
- Render playable MP4 and ZIP; no claim of completion until files validated.
- Separate **idea approval**, **production approval**, and **final publication approval**. Never auto-publish.

## Platform distribution: same finding, different invitation
- **TikTok:** first-frame payoff / human impact, concise narration, question that can generate an informed reply, appropriate caption keywords. Test against actual TikTok native data once the account connector is verified.
- **YouTube Shorts:** topic-specific searchable title, early action, real payoff, connect to related lessons where relevant. Track *engaged views*, *stayed to watch*, average view duration and percent viewed; raw views are not interchangeable with engagement after the 2026 metric changes.
- **Instagram Reels:** one strong first frame; clear original visual proof; practical takeaway worth saving/sharing. Measure watch time, retention, saves and shares by comparable age.
- **Threads:** the personal account is conversation-first. Lead with a genuine tradeoff in games, business, AI or entertainment; don't blanket crosspost every video. Select useful reactions as research leads, never as automatic publishing triggers.

## Two-week learning loop
Pilot six improvements as laid out in the retention audit. Use 24h / 48h / 7d checkpoints; capture video durations and post ages, not just total views. Analyze differences with a small-sample caveat. Primary outcome: median normalized viewing / stronger hold. Secondary: shares, saves, substantive comments. A loss of clarity to boost impressions is not success.

## Rubric for **user-supplied prompts** (evaluate before incorporation)
For each supplied prompt, classify:
1. **Adopt now:** directly improves hooks, relatable stakes, proof, humor, practical learning, review or measurement.
2. **Pilot:** potentially useful but unproven; specify one bounded test and success/failure measure.
3. **Modify:** good intent but bloated, generic, too algorithm-centric, costly, hard to verify or overproduced.
4. **Reject:** manipulative bait, made-up facts, copied creator voice/footage, false platform guarantees, inconsistent brand identity, unreviewed auto-publication or avoidable cost.
Report **what changes**, **why**, **what still needs evidence**, and **how success is measured**. Preserve final user approval on publishing.
