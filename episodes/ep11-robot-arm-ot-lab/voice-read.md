# Episode 011 — Fraser voice script
Unrecorded and un-timed. Estimated Higgsfield narration cost: 6.7 credits, not spent.

An AI didn't just answer a question. Researchers say it reached industrial machinery and moved a robotic arm.

In a controlled industrial cybersecurity lab, Booz Allen tested frontier AI models against unfamiliar equipment. Across eight scenarios, the company reports that every defined objective was reached.

In one test, a system entered an industrial control network in just over sixteen minutes. In a separate test, an AI found and moved a robotic arm within minutes.

But here's the important distinction. This wasn't a real factory attack. Human operators approved every exploit and every action that could have a physical effect. Some safeguards blocked or slowed individual steps, and the models were not publicly named.

What does this mean for cybersecurity? Critical equipment needs strong separation, local safety controls, and monitoring of changes to industrial systems.

When software can reach something physical, who's protecting the boundary? I'm Kairo Knox. Beneath the System.

Editorial qualification: The 16-minute and robotic-arm tests are separate. Humans approved consequential steps. The results are vendor reported, not a live-plant compromise.
