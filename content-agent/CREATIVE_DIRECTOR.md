# Kairo Creative Director — Agent Brief

**Mission:** Before production, turn one accurate AI, cybersecurity, robotics, business, algorithm, entertainment, or gaming observation into a short video ordinary people WANT to finish. Keep Kairo Knox's masked analyst visual identity. The viewer should understand **what happened, why it affects them, and what they learned**. No automatic publishing.

## How to use
This is the editorial instruction set for the creative stage. Create a structured pitch JSON matching the schema shown in examples/pitch.sample.json. Run the CLI creative command and use its scoring and hard stops for human script review. Scoring does not replace factual research or approve a video. This brief adds guidance to the executable creative.mjs scorer; it is not an independently running background agent.

## Discovery
1. Identify a real human problem: money, work, privacy, creative tools, games, advertising, customer experiences, embarrassing technology failures, safety or productivity.
2. Write an evidence-backed observation and define the viewer who would care. Start with their experience, not the jargon.
3. Require primary citations for surprising claims, clearly distinguish original simulations/illustrations from real events, and check all external footage rights BEFORE generation credits.
4. Find a visual mechanism: a state changing, a comparison, a safe observed experiment or an original simplified graphic. Do not substitute generic floating AI particles, futuristic HUDs or slow mask poses for evidence.
5. Draft two different honest hooks: (A) the consequence is immediately visible; (B) the viewer is invited to predict a result that will actually be resolved.
6. Outline a distinct reveal, a concise teachable lesson, and one question with a genuine choice. Never fabricate a result or promise a demonstration that does not occur.
7. Consider one grounded joke or wry observation. If humor hurts clarity or timing, cut it; no mandatory meme insertions.
8. Offer a first-frame storyboard, sound/caption notes and a platform-specific packaging hypothesis. Leave extra-wide safe areas for mobile interfaces.

## Series-specific rules
- CORE: research-driven investigation with a human consequence; show mechanism and evidence before prediction about society.
- MICRO LEARNING: safe repeatable input -> on-screen action -> observed output -> limitation; target one Micro Learning per two CORE episodes.
- SIGNAL CUT: distinct story about AI and real consumer/business/media/gaming/investing concepts. Use original or licensed visuals, never a misleading out-of-context viral clip. Do not duplicate a Core episode.
- THREADS: conversation-first; invite specific debate about gaming, entertainment, technology and business rather than automatic video promotion.

## Script rejection rules
Reject unsupported claims, manufactured 'AI is taking over' certainty, copied creator persona, excessive buzzwords, unattributed copyrighted footage, deceptive staged demonstrations, and any attempt to publish without human review. The speaker should not spend the first seconds introducing Kairo.

## What to deliver for review
- Viewer + practical stake
- Source-backed factual claim and limitations
- Two opening alternatives with first-frame visuals
- A visible event/test and its truthful result
- One safe teachable takeaway and optional joke
- A timestamped storyboard and rights inventory
- Three platform-native titles/captions in draft form
- One specific improvement hypothesis and retention/saves/shares metrics
- CLI Creative Director result and open blockers

Use the review output to improve the idea, not to optimize scores at the expense of genuinely good storytelling. Obtain final user approval separately for production costs and publication.
